# Style calibration

Use this when the user says the draft still sounds like AI, too neat, too poetic, too slogan-like, or unlike them.

## Inputs to compare

Ask for 3 to 10 human-written samples from the user:

- X posts
- replies
- notes
- diary fragments
- chat messages
- rough drafts

Do not only study polished posts. Rough notes often carry the real syntax.

## Extract syntax, not only themes

Look for:

- average sentence length
- where the user uses long sentences
- where they fragment a sentence
- repeated words
- filler phrases
- self-corrections
- sudden blunt endings
- casual intensifiers
- transitions between concrete scene and abstract judgment
- punctuation frequency
- line break rhythm

## Common AI tells to remove

- equal-length paragraphs
- every paragraph ending in a polished takeaway
- forced three-part lists
- abstract nouns stacked together
- motivational conclusion tone
- generic "people today" framing without a scene
- overly balanced contrasts
- neat quote-card sentences everywhere
- excessive Markdown structure in a post meant for social media

## Calibration process

1. Select one human-written sample closest to the target platform.
2. Summarize its rhythm in 5 to 8 concrete rules.
3. Mark 5 phrases that feel author-specific.
4. Mark 5 patterns that the author avoids or dislikes.
5. Rewrite the draft once for content.
6. Rewrite again for syntax.
7. Compare the final version against the human sample.

## What to preserve

Preserve:

- useful repetition
- mild awkwardness
- half-spoken transitions
- sudden personal admissions
- concrete physical details
- author's own metaphors
- weird but memorable phrasing

## What to change

Change:

- beautiful but generic sentences
- essay-like section symmetry
- polished inspirational endings
- claims with no scene
- explanations that sound like a lecture
- content-card rhythm when the target is a live X post

## Final check

Ask:

- Can I imagine this exact person typing this?
- Does the piece have one or two rough edges?
- Did it keep at least one original phrase from the user's raw answer?
- Does the ending feel like a person stopping, not a model concluding?
- Would the user feel slightly exposed by this draft?

If all answers are no, the draft is still too generic.
