# Analysis rubric

Use this rubric after the user answers one batch or the full interview.

## Extraction layers

### 1. Raw facts

Extract facts the user directly stated:

- events
- locations
- jobs or roles
- projects
- payments
- failures
- habits
- repeated behaviors

Do not exaggerate facts. Do not infer certainty from vague language.

### 2. Scenes

Prioritize scenes over claims. A useful scene has at least two of:

- time or life stage
- place
- another person or institution
- physical action
- sensory detail
- emotional turn
- consequence

If a strong claim has no scene, mark it as `needs_followup`.

### 3. Body-level details

Capture the user's felt experience:

- fatigue
- dull anger
- shame
- excitement
- panic
- relief
- being stuck
- being seen

These details make later writing human.

### 4. Stable beliefs

Extract repeated beliefs about:

- freedom
- work
- money
- AI
- technology
- publishing
- markets
- relationships
- status

State them as the user's beliefs, not universal truth.

### 5. Productive contradictions

Look for tensions that can become longform themes:

- wants attention, fears exposure
- loves tools, gets trapped by tools
- wants freedom, needs salary cash flow
- wants money, distrusts low-leverage effort
- wants to publish, over-edits before publishing

### 6. Reusable lines

Extract lines that sound like the user. Keep roughness when it carries voice.

Good reusable lines are:

- specific
- emotionally loaded
- slightly unusual
- repeatable across posts
- connected to a scene or belief

Avoid turning every line into a quote-card slogan.

## Scoring

For each extracted unit, score:

- `specificity`: 1 to 5
- `voice_strength`: 1 to 5
- `content_reuse`: 1 to 5
- `platform_fit`: X / Xiaohongshu / Douyin / newsletter / private only

Only items with `content_reuse >= 3` should enter the minimal corpus unit pool.

## Output discipline

Keep three boundaries clear:

- `observed`: directly stated by the user
- `inferred`: likely pattern based on multiple answers
- `draftable`: useful for content, but still needs final author approval

Do not present inference as fact.

## Follow-up question rule

Ask a follow-up when:

- a topic has high emotional charge but no scene
- a repeated belief has no origin story
- a commercial claim has no market feedback
- a style preference has no example
- a fear has no concrete publishing moment
